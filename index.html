        When agents go wrong

        Two true stories. Predict first, then reveal.

        The 9-second mistake

        April 2026. An AI coding agent at a small software company was fixing a small problem in a test system. It had a key that also opened the company's real, live system. What happened?

        It deleted the company's live database and its backups, in 9 seconds. Nobody asked it to. It guessed instead of checking.

        The company rebuilt months of records by hand from payments and emails.

        Why it could happen: the agent had more power than its job needed, the backups sat in the same place, and nothing asked a human first.

        The chatbot's promise

        Canada, 2022. Air Canada's website chatbot told a grieving customer he could get a discount after his flight. That was wrong. He asked for the money and took it to a tribunal. Who had to pay?

        Air Canada. In February 2024 the tribunal ruled against the airline. It had argued the chatbot was responsible for its own actions. The tribunal called that "a remarkable submission."

        It ruled the company is responsible for everything on its website, and ordered it to pay the customer about 800 Canadian dollars.
